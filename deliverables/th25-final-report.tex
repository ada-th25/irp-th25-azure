\begin{abstract}
Wildfires are destructive and a rapidly evolving hazard. With climate change, they become increasingly severe and frequent. This increases the need for a higher-resolution wildfire data for better monitoring and risk assessment. Geostationary satellites (GEO) have high-temporal resolution, but lacks the resolution needed for a detailed wildfire risk assessment. While low orbit satellites (LEO) provide better spatial resolution, but low temporal resolution to monitor rapid wildfire spread. Existing downscaling models have demonstrated their effectiveness in producing high-resolution remote sensing data. But many still suffers from artefacts, blurriness, and hallucinations. This project aims to achieve a high-resolution spatial downscaling of wildfire data by integrating additional physical covariates to the model, such as topography and landcover to mitigate physically implausible outputs. The project compares direct (2km to 30m) and cascaded (2km to 375m to 30m) downscaling pipelines and their effectiveness in producing high-fidelity, high-resolution wildfire data. The project compares diffusion (EDiffSR), attention U-Net (HAUNet), and transformer-based (ESTNet) downscaling models.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
In recent years, due to rising popularity in Net-Zero initiatives, carbon investors are increasingly funding carbon reduction and removal projects, such as reforestation (\cite{InvestmentTrendsOutcomes}). However, these carbon projects are at risk because wildfires can destroy and release the carbon back into the atmosphere. With climate change, wildfires are becoming increasingly severe and frequent (\cite{jonesClimateChangeIncreases,}). This causes economic losses for the insurance industry. For instance, The January 2025 Los Angeles County wildfires set an all-time record, causing \$40 billion in insured losses alone from a single event (\cite{staffHowWildfiresStorms2026}). This project, conducted with Artio, aims to address this gap. By providing highly accurate risk assessment models, this research seeks to give better confidence and enable better risk assessment.

High-spatial and high-temporal resolution wildfire data is highly desired in risk assessment. However, high-temporal resolution data obtained from geostationary satellites (GEO) have limited spatial resolution, which makes them difficult to use for accurate risk assessment. While low orbit satellites (LEO) offers high-spatial resolution, but low-temporal resolution.

To bridge this gap, existing methods uses Deep Learning (DL) models for downscaling due to their ability in recognising non-linear spatiotemporal patterns. However, the resolution gap between GEO and LEO is significant (2km - 30m). This increases the risk of producing physically implausible or unrealistic outputs, as a single low-resolution (LR) pixel can correspond to different high-resolution (HR) details (\cite{wangDeepLearningImage2020}). In this project, to mitigate this limitation, the model integrates additional physical information, such as HR topography and landcover to help guide the downscaling process. To further improve downscaling, the project aims to investigate whether cascaded downscaling (2km to 375m to 30m) produces more accurate and better results compared to direct downscaling (2km to 30m). By breaking up the downscaling process, the model trains on a smaller resolution gap, aiming to mitigate physically implausible outputs. This project will compare the cascaded and direct pipeline performance of different downscaling architectures, including hybrid attention U-Net, a transformer-based model, and a diffusion-based model.

\section{Data and Materials}

\subsection{Input Datasets}
\begin{itemize}
    \item GOES-17 channel 7 (IR shortwave) of Level 1B (L1B) Radiances product (ABI-L1B-Rad). Has spatial resolution of 2km at every 5 mins. This channel is selected because it is commonly used to identify hot regions (\cite{badhanDeepLearningApproach2024}). This will be the main coarse input.
    \item Copernicus DEM GLO-30: Global 30m Digital Elevation Model with 30m resolution (\cite{europeanspaceagencyCopernicus2022}). DEM band will be used.
    \item ESA WorldCover 10m v100 landcover dataset with 10m resolution. This dataset have 11 land cover classes, including tree cover, shrubland, grassland, and permanent water bodies (\cite{zanagaESAWorldCover102021}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Target Fire Data}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Landsat-8 active fire dataset includes data for fire scenes taken from August and September 2020 with dimensions 256x256 at 30m resolution (\cite{dealmeidapereiraActiveFireDetection2021}). This will be the final target HR target data. Fire-sensitive SWIR and thermal bands will be used.
    \item  VIIRS (S-NPP) Band Active Fire with 375 m spatial resolution (\cite{earthsciencedatasystemsVIIRSSNPPBand2025}) will be used as the target data for the 2km to 375m stage downscaling. The I-4 channel brightness temperature (3.55-3.93 µm) band will be used.
\end{itemize}

\section{Methods}
\subsection{HAUNet (Hybrid Attention U-Net)}
HAUNet is a hybrid attention U-Net model (\cite{wangHybridAttentionBasedUShaped2023}) that uses two variants of attention blocks:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{CEM}: Applies channel attention.
    \item \textbf{S\_CEM}: Additionally applies spatial attention in parallel.
\end{itemize}

The model uses cross-scale attention module as skip connections across different scale outputs in the encoder, to capture dependency between different scales. The overall framework of wildfire-adapted HAUNet is shown in Figure \ref{fig:haunet}. The original HAUNet S\_CEM does full spatial attention, which is $O((H\cdot W)^2)$ and will run into OOM crashes. Hence the input GOES and covariates are sampled to a smaller image before feeding into the model. But this loses covariates' high-resolution information, so an additional covariate pyramid encoder was added form the original model to run in parallel. This performs windowed spatial attention on the covariates at fine resolution. The output of the covariate encoders are fused at different scales during refinement.
